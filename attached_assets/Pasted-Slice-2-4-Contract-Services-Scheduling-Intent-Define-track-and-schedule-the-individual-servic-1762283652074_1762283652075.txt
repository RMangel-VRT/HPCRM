Slice 2.4 — Contract Services & Scheduling

Intent
Define, track, and schedule the individual services that are included within each contract so they can generate work tickets and completion records throughout the year.
This turns a contract from a dollar figure into a working maintenance plan.

Scope — what users should be able to do
A) Service Setup (inside each Contract)

When viewing a Contract, users can:

Add or remove Included Services from a predefined service catalog (your list below).

For each service, define:

Service name (from list)

Service frequency or count (e.g., 26 visits, 2 cleanups)

Seasonal window (months of year or date range)

Notes (special instructions or exclusions)

Core service types (template list):

Full mowing service

Includes mowing, trimming, blowing

26 visits (2 in April, 2 in October, weekly May–September)

Pet station service

Specify number of stations and visits per week

Chemical service

Option: Organic or Non-organic

Sub-services:

Roundup or organic equivalent (weekly, on mow day)

Broadleaf (4× per year)

Fertilizer (1 spring pre-emergent, 2 summer, 1 fall)

Shrub trimming

1 summer service, 1 fall service

Ornamental grass trimming

1 fall service

Aeration

1 spring service

Cleanups

1 spring, 1 fall

Tree pruning

1 service, up to 10 ft

(This catalog should be reusable so every contract can reference the same definitions.)

B) Scheduling Layer

Each selected service defines how many tickets need to be created and when.

Example:

Mowing: 26 services → generate 26 scheduled tickets (2 April, 2 October, weekly May–Sept).

Shrub trimming: 2 tickets (summer, fall).

Pet station: recurring weekly tickets, number = (stations × visits per week × weeks in season).

Chemical service: generates separate tickets for each application type.

Each ticket includes:

Service name

Contract (and customer) reference

Target date (auto-generated but editable)

Assigned crew or left unassigned

Status (Scheduled / Completed / Skipped / Cancelled)

Notes / photos / attachments (optional)

C) User experience

On a Contract:

“Services & Schedule” tab shows all included services and how many tickets each creates.

Button: Generate Schedule → populates tickets for the season based on frequencies.

Users can adjust individual dates or delete unwanted tickets before confirming.

On a Customer or Operations view:

“Calendar” or “Ticket Board” view lists all tickets by date or service type.

Users can mark tickets Completed, add notes/photos, or reschedule.

Rules & behavior

Tickets auto-generate based on service rules but can be manually added or edited.

Each ticket always links back to its parent Service → Contract → Customer chain.

Changing the contract’s start or end date should prompt a “regenerate schedule?” option.

If a service is removed from a contract, its future tickets are automatically cancelled (keep completion history intact).

Permissions

Admin / Office: can create, edit, and regenerate service schedules; mark complete.

Ops: can view their assigned tickets and mark them completed with notes/photos.

Viewer: read-only visibility.

Acceptance Criteria

In a contract, users can select which services are included and define their details.

Clicking Generate Schedule creates the correct number of tickets with appropriate dates.

Tickets show up in the operations view, grouped by date or service type.

Marking a ticket “Completed” stores the completion date and notes.

Editing the contract’s date range offers the option to regenerate its schedule.

Removing a service cancels any uncompleted tickets tied to it.

Completion data is retained for reporting and audit.

Test Checklist

Add mowing and cleanup to a contract → generate schedule → confirm ticket counts and dates align with the rules.

Add chemical services → verify distinct tickets for Roundup, Broadleaf, and Fertilizer with correct months.

Mark some tickets complete, others skipped → ensure tracking works.

Adjust contract end date → prompted to adjust schedule.

Remove one service → its future tickets disappear but past completions remain.

Ops user can view their assigned tickets and mark completion; cannot edit other data.

Not included yet

Crew assignment logic or route optimization (later).

Automated reminders or notifications.

PDF service reports or export (future).
